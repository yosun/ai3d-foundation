## What we mean by 3D

At AI3D Foundation, **3D is not limited to three-dimensional geometry.**

*It began there.*

We started with a simple problem: make 3D creation more intuitive and create better interfaces for human-AI co-creation.

But working with spatial computation exposed a larger idea.

Most software reduces interaction to predefined representations: prompts, pixels, files, parameters, timelines, windows, and interfaces.

We use **3D** to describe the richer computational space beyond those constraints.

3D can contain:

- space and structure
- relationships and correspondences
- motion and time
- transformations and constraints
- context and situations
- objects, environments, and worlds
- possibilities and alternative states
- relations we have not yet learned how to represent

> A dimension does not have to be a spatial axis. It can represent a degree of freedom through which a system can vary, relate, transform, or act.
>
> **So 3D is not a dimensional limit.**

It is our shorthand for **dimensional computation**: computation that preserves and operates through the structure of a problem rather than prematurely flattening it into a prompt, pixel array, file, or conventional interface.

AI can generate through these dimensions.

Humans can interact through them.

Agents can reason and act through them.

Different representations can translate between them.

And new dimensions can emerge as we invent new computational primitives.

**AI3D explores what becomes possible when intelligence has more dimensions through which to compute.**
